An invented example

My manager cut me off twice in Tuesday's planning meeting while I was presenting. At the end he gave my Q1 project to Dana, in front of everyone. He doesn't respect me. He thinks Dana is better than me.

Happened

Cut off twice while presenting, Tuesday.

The Q1 project given to Dana, in front of the team.

Told

Nothing yet.

Concluded

He doesn't respect me.

He thinks Dana is better than me.

Advice built on the first group holds up. Advice built on the third usually doesn't.

Two ways this could be true.

About them

He is not valuing your work. Two interruptions and a reassignment in one meeting fit that story.

About the setup

The meeting was against a deadline and he was managing the clock. Dana's team had room this quarter and yours is carrying the launch.

The facts fit both. One question would tell them apart.

What it won't do

Take your side by default.

Tell you what the other person is thinking.

Pretend one conversation proves anything.

Give conversation advice when the problem is conduct. Then it points you to the people whose job it is.

What you leave with

Ask the reason, privately

Within two weeks I will have heard his reason for moving the project, in his own words.

Check on Monday, November 2

One step. One sentence you'd see if it worked. One date.
